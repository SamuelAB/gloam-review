Gloam VR: work in progress

Gloam (Entre chien et loup), an immersive VR installation by Samuel Arsenault-Brassard.

Gloam is a vast cave, hand-sculpted in virtual reality, developed over four years. Inside, a complete cycle of natural light moves through the architecture, from dawn to midday to twilight to dark and back again. The visitor walks freely through the space with nothing to hold and nothing to press: just the body, the architecture, and the shifting light. The lighting is computed offline at a fidelity no real-time system can match, then streamed and played back inside the headset, giving the cave the weight and presence of a real place, yet holding the traces of a new digital matter. The work draws on Louis Kahn's natural light, James Turrell's light installations, and Edmund Burke's philosophy of the sublime.

These pages follow the work in progress as the piece becomes a living place: weather (rain that behaves like real water on real stone, wind, dust that settles and is partly washed away), a ground under the sculpture shaped by years of that weather, and a custom real-time player built for it. Every behaviour comes from physics and published measurements rather than effects tuned by eye, and the method, what was tried and what was set aside are kept here as a record.
