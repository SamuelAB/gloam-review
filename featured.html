Gloam: what it looks like now

Gloam (Entre chien et loup), an immersive VR installation by Samuel Arsenault-Brassard: a vast cave, hand-sculpted in virtual reality over four years, in which a full cycle of natural light moves through the architecture. These are recent images of the work as it becomes a living place: an old ruined floor, rain on real stone, dust in the sunbeams, walls that breathe, and small animals that live there.

All images are drawn in Gloam's own engine, in its light. Most are brightened for screens, as noted; the cave itself is dimmer. Work in progress continues on the current work page.

The cave and its light

Rain

Rain that behaves like real water on real stone: where it falls through the openings, how it runs and pools, how the stone darkens as it gets wet.

Creatures (first looks)

Small animals live on the floor and react to the visitor, fleeing a hand or a head that comes too close. These are first looks, still being refined.
